SLIDE 2: Problem statement  [0:20-0:55 (35 s)]

Say:
Sixty schools are sending 1,244 people to the same stadium. If every school books its own bus, that's 60 buses, and only 68.0% of the seats are filled. Sharing is obviously cheaper, but schools differ in size and location, buses come in three sizes, and there are far too many groupings to check by hand. And each school's group must stay together on one bus, because one adult supervises it.

Key message for judges: Booking separately wastes seats and money; sharing is too complex to plan by hand.

Transition: So here's how Campus Cruise plans it automatically.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SLIDE 3: Proposed solution  [0:55-1:25 (30 s)]

Say:
Campus Cruise works in six steps. Load and check the data. Pick how buses are priced. OR-Tools decides which schools share each bus and the pickup order. Then separate code re-checks every rule and recalculates every cost, and a plan that fails is never shown. Finally, the results go to a dashboard and map, and can be downloaded.

Key message for judges: Every plan is optimised, then independently checked, before anyone sees it.

Transition: Let me show what's inside the optimise step.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SLIDE 4: Algorithms and AI approach  [1:25-2:10 (45 s)]

Say:
The challenge prices buses three ways. With a fixed price per bus, location doesn't matter, so it's a packing problem: OR-Tools' CP-SAT solver finds the cheapest plan and proves nothing cheaper exists. When price depends on miles or driving time, pickup order matters, so we use vehicle-routing search. It finds very good plans, but we never call them optimal. The rules are hard in every model: no school split, no bus over capacity. To be precise: this is optimisation, not a trained model. Generative AI is a separate assistant, and the machine-learning model is experimental.

Key message for judges: Exact optimisation where it can be proven; honest 'best found' labels where it cannot.

Transition: So what does that achieve on the real data?

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SLIDE 5: Results and performance  [2:10-2:45 (35 s)]

Say:
With a fixed price per bus, the plan costs 1,875 instead of 3,330: 43.7% less, with 25 buses instead of 60. That is provably the cheapest. The other two models save 28.6% and 17.7%; those are the best plans found in five minutes. Each model has its own prices, so I only compare within a model, and these are cost units, not pounds.

Key message for judges: 43.7% cheaper and 35 fewer buses, proven optimal for fixed pricing.

Transition: Let me show you the app producing this.

SLIDE 8: Sector impact and beneficiaries  [4:40-5:15 (35 s)]

Who gains? Organisers get a checked plan in seconds. Schools and pupils pay less to attend. Coach operators get a clear booking list, and venues have fewer coaches to park. SLIDE 9: Responsible AI and Welsh strategic alignment  [5:15-5:50 (35 s)]

Campus Cruise is decision support, not an autonomous system. Every plan says whether it's proven or just the best found. It uses school-level head counts only, no names. The organiser approves every AI change, and independent code re-checks every rule.
